Bit Flip

A game of memory and interference for 2–5 players. About 20 minutes.

Components

  • A bag of small blocks: 32 white and 32 black (40 each with five players).
  • A deck of double-sided goal cards, white on one face and black on the other: 48 (56 with five players).
  • One memory board per player: three rows of 2, 3 and 4 slots (the registers).
  • A deck of secret pattern cards: every pattern of 2, 3 and 4 bits (28 cards).
  • A deck of 20 action cards (24 with five players): 5 each of Bit Flip, Rehearsal, Priming, Recount.
  • A score track or paper.

The idea

The goal cards in the middle are what the world asks for: a 2-bit pattern, a 3-bit pattern and a 4-bit pattern. You draw blocks from the bag and either commit them to your memory board or spend them as votes that change the goals. Fill a register so it matches its goal, left to right, and you score it. Wider registers are worth more, take longer, and give the table more time to vote the goal out from under you.

Setup

  1. Shuffle the goal deck. Deal cards face down into three rows of 2, 3 and 4, then turn each over to whichever face comes up. This is the goal layout.
  2. Everyone takes a memory board and one secret pattern card, kept hidden.
  3. Shuffle the action deck. Choose a first player. In a two-player game the second player draws three blocks on their first turn instead of two.

Your turn

  1. Draw two blocks from the bag without looking.
  2. Place every block you drew. A block goes either into an empty slot on your board, or onto any goal card as a vote. You may also pick up blocks from your unfinished rows and place them again, anywhere, as part of this.
  3. Any row that is now full locks: its blocks can't be moved again.
    • If the locked row matches its goal, or matches your secret pattern, it scores immediately: 3 points for the 2-register, 6 for the 3, 10 for the 4. Return its blocks to the bag and redeal that goal from the deck; the votes on the old goal cards go back in the bag. If you used your secret, draw a new one.
    • If it doesn't match, it stays locked as a bet. It scores the moment the goal changes to match it, on anyone's turn.
  4. Jackpot: when you score the 4-register, don't return its four blocks to the bag. Place as many of them as you like on goal cards as votes, right now; the rest go back in the bag.
  5. Scoring the 3- or the 4-register also draws you an action card. Action cards can be played at any time on your turn.

Goal cards and votes

A goal card shows the colour with more blocks on it. With no blocks, or a tie, it shows its printed face. So one block flips a fresh card, and it takes more to flip it back. Votes stay until that goal is scored and redealt.

Interference

When your vote flips a goal card, memory gets overwritten: every block of the old colour sitting in that position of an unfinished row, on anyone's board, is removed. Take one of the removed blocks into your hand and place it this turn; the rest go back in the bag. Blocks already showing the new colour are untouched, and locked rows are never affected.

Action cards

CardEffect
Bit FlipFlip one block on any board, or turn over one goal card (its votes return to the bag).
RehearsalDraw three extra blocks and place them now.
PrimingYour next draw is four blocks; keep two, return two.
RecountReturn up to two blocks of one colour from one goal card to the bag.

End of the game

When a scored goal can't be fully redealt because the deck is empty, the game is ending: finish the round so everyone has had the same number of turns. The cards of that goal are removed but its votes stay where they are and alone decide the pattern: each position is the colour with more votes, and a tied position is wild (matches either colour). Such a goal can still be scored by anyone during the final round, and is not redealt.

Highest score wins. Ties go to the player who placed fewer votes during the game.

Tips

  • A block of the wrong colour isn't waste. It's a vote, or a bet that the goal will flip.
  • Lock a 4-row early and the whole table will try to flip its goal. Lock it late and someone may beat you to the jackpot.
  • Watch the bag. If the table hoards white, the draws go black.

Variants

  • Calm: play without Interference.
  • Grid: the left-aligned columns of your board (4, 3 and 2 slots) also count as registers of their width. A block in any full line is locked.
  • Short: use 36 goal cards.
